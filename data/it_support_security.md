# IT Support and Security Policy

> Synthetic demonstration policy - not an actual company policy. Fictional content written for a software engineering exercise.

**Document owner:** Technology and Security
**Effective date:** 1 January 2026
**Review cycle:** Annual

## Purpose

This policy explains how employees get IT support, what to do when a company laptop develops a fault, how to report a lost or stolen device, how to recognise and report phishing and other suspicious messages, and how to escalate a suspected security incident. It applies to all staff issued with company IT equipment or system access.

## 1. Service desk and support channels

The IT Service Desk is the first point of contact for any technology problem, from a forgotten password to a printer that won't connect. Requests can be logged through the internal support portal or by emailing the service desk directly; urgent issues that stop someone working can also be phoned in.

When logging a request, include the device name or asset tag, a short description of the problem, and anything that was happening immediately before it started - this is usually enough for the service desk to triage the request without a back-and-forth. Most routine requests, such as software access or a peripheral not being recognised, are picked up within one working day.

## 2. Laptop faults and hardware problems

If a company laptop is running slowly, won't turn on, has a cracked screen, a failing battery, or any other hardware or performance problem, log a fault with the Service Desk rather than attempting a repair. Employees should not open the case, replace internal components, or take the device to a third-party repair shop, since this can void the warranty and, in some cases, compromise the device's security configuration.

Where possible, describe the specific symptom - for example "screen has a vertical crack" or "battery drains within an hour" - rather than a general "not working." This helps the technician bring the right replacement part or loan device. A loan laptop is normally provided while a fault is being fixed if the employee cannot otherwise work.

## 3. Lost or stolen devices

A lost or stolen company laptop, phone, or other device must be reported to the Service Desk immediately, and the employee's manager should also be told as soon as practical. Do not wait to see if the device turns up before reporting it - the priority is protecting company data and accounts, not the hardware itself.

As soon as a report is received, IT Security will attempt to remotely lock the device and, if it cannot be recovered quickly, may remotely wipe it to protect any company data stored locally. The employee will usually be asked to change their account password, and may be asked whether the device was signed in to any company systems at the time it went missing and roughly when and where it was lost, since this helps assess what may be at risk.

If a device was stolen rather than simply lost, employees are encouraged - though not required - to report the theft to the police, particularly if a crime reference number would help with an insurance claim. That is a separate, personal decision and should never delay the internal report above.

## 4. Phishing and suspicious messages

Phishing emails and text messages try to trick employees into clicking a malicious link, opening an infected attachment, or entering their username and password on a fake login page. Suspicious signs include an unexpected sense of urgency, a sender address that doesn't quite match who it claims to be from, and links that don't go where the visible text suggests.

If a message looks suspicious, do not click any links or open any attachments. Suspected phishing should be reported using the "report phishing" option in the email client, or forwarded to the security mailbox if that option isn't available. If an employee has already clicked a link or entered a password on a page they now believe was fake, they should report it straight away rather than waiting - reporting promptly is treated as the responsible action, not something to be penalised for.

## 5. Security incidents and data exposure

A security incident is any situation where company data, systems, or accounts may have been accessed, shared, or exposed without authorisation - for example a suspected account compromise, an email sent to the wrong recipient with sensitive information attached, or a device found unlocked and unattended. Suspected incidents should be reported to IT Security as soon as they are noticed.

When reporting, include what happened, when it was noticed, and which systems, accounts, or data might be affected; the security team will confirm what else, if anything, is needed. Employees should not attempt to investigate or "fix" a suspected compromise on their own, since that can make it harder to understand what actually happened and to contain it properly.

## 6. Using equipment away from the office

Company laptops and other issued equipment may be used away from the office, including at home or while travelling, provided the same basic precautions apply as in the office: the device should not be left unattended in a public place, and screens should be locked when stepping away. Any fault, loss, or theft while working away from the office is reported in exactly the same way as described above.
