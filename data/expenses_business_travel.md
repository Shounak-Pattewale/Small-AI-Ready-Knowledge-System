# Expenses and Business Travel Policy

> Synthetic demonstration policy - not an actual company policy. Fictional content written for a software engineering exercise.

**Document owner:** Finance
**Effective date:** 1 January 2026
**Review cycle:** Annual

## Purpose

This policy explains how employees claim back reasonable costs incurred for business purposes, and what is expected when travelling for work, including travel to attend external training or conferences.

## 1. Submitting claims

Business expenses should be submitted through the expenses system as soon as practical after they are incurred, ideally within the same month. Each claim needs a brief business reason - what the cost was for and why it was necessary - and a copy of the receipt or invoice; claims submitted without evidence may be delayed while the missing information is requested.

Claims are reviewed by the employee's manager, who checks that the cost was reasonable and genuinely business-related before approving it. Once approved, reimbursement is paid with the employee's next normal payroll run. Employees should raise any question about whether a particular cost is claimable with their manager before incurring it, rather than after the event.

## 2. Rail travel

Standard-class rail travel should normally be used for UK business journeys, booked in advance through the approved travel booking channel where one is available, since advance booking is usually significantly cheaper than buying on the day. First-class travel is not part of standard policy and requires the traveller's manager to approve it in advance, based on a genuine business reason such as needing to work confidentially during the journey.

Where a journey involves an early start, a late finish, or a change of trains that makes standing for part of the journey likely, employees should mention this when booking so a suitable service can be chosen, rather than assuming a higher class of travel will automatically be approved.

## 3. Other travel and subsistence

Reasonable costs for taxis, parking, and overnight accommodation are claimable when they are a genuine part of a business trip - for example, a taxi taken late at night for safety reasons, or a hotel booked because a same-day return isn't practical. Accommodation should be booked at a reasonable standard for the purpose of the trip rather than the most expensive option available, and meals taken while travelling can be claimed with a receipt as part of the same trip.

## 4. Training courses and conferences

Travel and subsistence costs for attending an approved external training course or a work-related conference are claimed in exactly the same way as any other business trip - through the expenses system, with a receipt and a brief note of the event. Attendance itself should already have manager approval before the event (see the Learning and Training policy for how that approval works); the travel costs to get there and back follow this expenses policy.

If a conference is overseas, the same general expectations apply: standard-class travel where practical, a reasonable standard of accommodation, and manager approval in advance given the additional cost involved in international travel.
